CHAPTER 1: THE FIVE PILLARS OF ISLAM
═══════════════════════════════════

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said: "Islam is built on five pillars: the testimony that there is no god but Allah and Muhammad is His Messenger, establishing prayer, paying Zakat, performing Hajj, and fasting in Ramadan." (Sahih Bukhari 8, Sahih Muslim 16)

1. SHAHADAH (Declaration of Faith)
   Arabic: أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله وأشهد أن محمدا رسول الله
   Transliteration: Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah
   Meaning: I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.
   Conditions: Knowledge, certainty, acceptance, compliance, truthfulness, sincerity, love.
   Ruling: This is the gateway to Islam. Without sincere belief in this declaration, no other act of worship is accepted.

2. SALAH (Prayer) — See Chapter 3 for detailed rulings.
3. ZAKAT (Charity) — See Chapter 4.
4. SAWM (Fasting) — See Chapter 5.
5. HAJJ (Pilgrimage) — See Chapter 6.

CHAPTER 2: PURIFICATION (TAHARAH)
═══════════════════════════════

"Allah loves those who purify themselves." (Quran 9:108)
"Cleanliness is half of faith." (Sahih Muslim 223)

2.1 WUDU (Ablution)
---
Obligatory Acts of Wudu (agreed upon by all four madhabs):
1. Washing the face (from forehead to chin, ear to ear)
2. Washing both arms including the elbows
3. Wiping over the head (Masah)
4. Washing both feet including the ankles
5. Intention (Niyyah) — Hanafi: recommended; Maliki/Shafi'i/Hanbali: obligatory
6. Performing acts in order (Tartib) — Shafi'i/Hanbali: obligatory; Hanafi/Maliki: recommended

Sunnah Acts of Wudu:
- Saying "Bismillah" before starting
- Washing the hands three times
- Using Miswak (tooth-stick)
- Rinsing the mouth (Madmadah) three times
- Sniffing water into the nose (Istinshaq) three times
- Washing each part three times
- Running wet fingers through the beard
- Starting with the right side
- Wiping the ears

Nullifiers of Wudu (agreed upon by all madhabs):
1. Anything exiting from the front or back passage (urine, stool, gas/flatulence)
2. Loss of consciousness (sleep in a reclining position, fainting)
3. Touching the private parts directly (Shafi'i/Hanbali: breaks wudu; Hanafi: does not)
4. Vomiting a mouthful (Hanafi: breaks wudu; Shafi'i: does not)

Dua after Wudu:
Arabic: أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله وحده لا شريك له وأشهد أن محمدا عبده ورسوله
Reference: Sahih Muslim 234

2.2 GHUSL (Full Body Purification)
---
Ghusl becomes obligatory after:
1. Sexual intercourse (Janabah) — even without ejaculation
2. Ejaculation during sleep (wet dream) or while awake
3. Menstruation (Hayd) — upon completion of the period
4. Postnatal bleeding (Nifas) — upon completion
5. Death (Ghusl of the deceased is Fard Kifayah)
6. Embracing Islam (recommended by majority, obligatory per Hanbali)

Steps of Ghusl (Sunnah method):
1. Make intention (Niyyah)
2. Say "Bismillah"
3. Wash both hands three times
4. Wash the private parts
5. Perform complete Wudu
6. Pour water over the head three times, running fingers through the hair
7. Wash the entire body starting with the right side
8. Ensure water reaches every part of the body

2.3 TAYAMMUM (Dry Purification)
---
Permitted when water is unavailable or its use would cause harm.
Evidence: "If you are ill or on a journey... and you cannot find water, then purify yourselves with clean earth." (Quran 4:43)

Method:
1. Make intention
2. Strike the earth with both hands
3. Wipe the face with the palms
4. Wipe the hands to the wrists (Hanafi: to the elbows)

CHAPTER 3: SALAH (PRAYER)
═══════════════════════════════

"Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times." (Quran 4:103)

3.1 The Five Daily Prayers
---
| Prayer | Arabic | Rakats (Fard) | Sunnah Before | Sunnah After |
|--------|--------|---------------|---------------|--------------|
| Fajr   | الفجر  | 2             | 2             | 0            |
| Dhuhr  | الظهر  | 4             | 4 (or 2)      | 2            |
| Asr    | العصر  | 4             | 0 (or 4)      | 0            |
| Maghrib| المغرب | 3             | 0             | 2            |
| Isha   | العشاء | 4             | 0 (or 4)      | 2 + Witr     |

Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever prays 12 Rak'at of Sunnah prayers during the day and night, a house will be built for him in Paradise." (Sahih Muslim 728)

3.2 Conditions for Prayer (Shurut as-Salah)
---
1. Islam — must be Muslim
2. Sanity — must be of sound mind
3. Puberty — prayer becomes obligatory at puberty
4. Purity — must have Wudu and be free from major impurity
5. Covering the 'Awrah — Men: navel to knee; Women: entire body except face and hands (majority view)
6. Facing the Qiblah — direction of the Kaaba in Makkah
7. Time — prayer must be within its designated time
8. Intention (Niyyah)

3.3 Pillars (Arkan) of Prayer
---
1. Standing (Qiyam) — if able
2. Opening Takbir: "Allahu Akbar" (الله أكبر)
3. Reciting Al-Fatiha in every Rak'ah
4. Ruku' (Bowing) — saying "Subhana Rabbiyal Adheem" (سبحان ربي العظيم)
5. Rising from Ruku' — "Sami'Allahu liman hamidah" (سمع الله لمن حمده)
6. Sujud (Prostration) — saying "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la" (سبحان ربي الأعلى)
   Prophet ﷺ said: "The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so increase your supplications therein." (Sahih Muslim 482)
7. Sitting between two Sujud
8. The final Tashahhud (sitting)
9. Sending Salawat upon the Prophet ﷺ in the final sitting
10. Tasleem: "Assalamu Alaikum wa Rahmatullah" (السلام عليكم ورحمة الله)

3.4 Sujud as-Sahw (Prostration of Forgetfulness)
---
Required when a worshipper makes an unintentional mistake during prayer:
- Adding an extra Rak'ah
- Omitting a Wajib (obligatory) act
- Doubt about the number of Rak'at prayed
Method: Two extra prostrations before or after the Tasleem (varies by madhab).

3.5 Jumu'ah (Friday Prayer)
---
"O you who have believed, when [the adhan] is called for prayer on the day of Jumu'ah, proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade." (Quran 62:9)
- Obligatory on every free, adult, sane, resident Muslim male
- 2 Rak'at with two Khutbahs (sermons) before it
- The Prophet ﷺ said: "The best day on which the sun rises is Friday." (Sahih Muslim 854)

3.6 Salat al-Janazah (Funeral Prayer)
---
Fard Kifayah (communal obligation): if some Muslims perform it, others are absolved.
Method: 4 Takbirs, no Ruku' or Sujud.
- After 1st Takbir: Recite Al-Fatiha
- After 2nd Takbir: Send Salawat upon the Prophet ﷺ
- After 3rd Takbir: Make Dua for the deceased
- After 4th Takbir: Tasleem

CHAPTER 4: ZAKAT
═══════════════════════════════

"And establish prayer and give Zakat." (Quran 2:43)

4.1 Conditions for Zakat Being Obligatory
---
1. Islam
2. Freedom (not enslaved)
3. Full ownership of wealth
4. Wealth reaching Nisab threshold
5. One full lunar year (Hawl) has passed on the wealth
6. Wealth being in excess of basic needs

4.2 Nisab Thresholds
---
- Gold: 87.48 grams (approximately 7.5 tolas)
- Silver: 612.36 grams (approximately 52.5 tolas)
- Cash: equivalent to the value of silver Nisab (scholars differ; many use silver for the benefit of the poor)
- Rate: 2.5% of net qualifying wealth

4.3 Wealth Subject to Zakat
---
- Gold and silver
- Cash and bank balances
- Trade goods and business inventory
- Stocks and investments
- Agricultural produce (rate: 5% if irrigated, 10% if rain-fed)
- Livestock (specific thresholds apply)
- Mined minerals

4.4 The Eight Categories of Zakat Recipients
---
"Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect it and for bringing hearts together and for freeing captives and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the stranded traveler." (Quran 9:60)

1. Al-Fuqara (The Poor) — those who lack basic necessities
2. Al-Masakin (The Needy) — those who have some but not enough
3. Al-Amilina Alayha (Zakat Collectors/Administrators)
4. Al-Mu'allafatu Qulubuhum (Those whose hearts are to be reconciled)
5. Fir-Riqab (Freeing captives/slaves)
6. Al-Gharimin (Those in debt — who cannot repay)
7. Fi Sabilillah (In the cause of Allah)
8. Ibn as-Sabil (The stranded traveler)

4.5 Zakat al-Fitr
---
- Obligatory on every Muslim at the end of Ramadan
- Amount: One Sa' (approximately 2.5–3 kg) of staple food
- Must be given before Eid prayer
- "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ enjoined Zakat al-Fitr as purification for the fasting person." (Abu Dawud 1609)

CHAPTER 5: SAWM (FASTING)
═══════════════════════════════

"O you who have believed, fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, so that you may achieve Taqwa." (Quran 2:183)

5.1 Obligatory Fasts
---
- The month of Ramadan
- Making up missed fasts of Ramadan
- Fasts of expiation (Kaffarah)
- Fasts due to vows (Nadhr)

5.2 Conditions That Obligate Fasting
---
1. Islam
2. Puberty (children are encouraged but not obligated)
3. Sanity
4. Good health (the sick may delay)
5. Residency (travelers may delay)
6. For women: absence of menstruation and postnatal bleeding

5.3 Pillars of Fasting
---
1. Intention (Niyyah) — must be made before Fajr for obligatory fasts
2. Abstaining from food, drink, and sexual relations from Fajr to Maghrib

5.4 What Breaks the Fast
---
1. Eating or drinking intentionally
2. Intentional vomiting
3. Sexual intercourse
4. Menstruation or postnatal bleeding
5. Injection of nourishing substances (IV drip, etc.)
Things that do NOT break the fast:
- Unintentional eating/drinking (out of forgetfulness) — "If someone forgets and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast, for it is Allah who fed him." (Sahih Bukhari 1933)
- Brushing teeth (without swallowing toothpaste)
- Blood tests (small amount of blood withdrawal)
- Eye drops, ear drops (debated; many scholars say they do not break the fast)

5.5 Recommended (Sunnah) Fasts
---
- Mondays and Thursdays — "The Prophet ﷺ used to fast on Monday and Thursday." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 745)
- Three days of each month (13th, 14th, 15th — Ayyam al-Bid)
- Day of Arafah (9th Dhul-Hijjah for non-pilgrims) — "Fasting on the Day of Arafah expiates the sins of the preceding year and the coming year." (Sahih Muslim 1162)
- Day of Ashura (10th Muharram) — with the 9th or 11th
- Six days of Shawwal — "Whoever fasts Ramadan and follows it with six days of Shawwal, it is as if he fasted the entire year." (Sahih Muslim 1164)

CHAPTER 6: HAJJ AND UMRAH
═══════════════════════════════

"And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House — for whoever is able to find thereto a way." (Quran 3:97)

6.1 Conditions for Hajj Being Obligatory
---
1. Islam
2. Sanity
3. Puberty
4. Financial and physical ability
5. Safety of the route
6. For women: a Mahram (male relative escort) — majority view

6.2 Types of Hajj
---
1. Ifrad: Hajj only (no Umrah)
2. Tamattu': Umrah first, then Hajj (most common for overseas pilgrims). Sacrifice required.
3. Qiran: Hajj and Umrah combined with one Ihram. Sacrifice required.

6.3 Pillars (Arkan) of Hajj
---
1. Ihram (entering the sacred state with intention)
2. Wuquf at Arafat (standing at Arafat on 9th Dhul-Hijjah)
   "Hajj is Arafat." (Abu Dawud 1949, Tirmidhi 889)
3. Tawaf al-Ifadah (circumambulation of the Kaaba after Arafat)
4. Sa'i between Safa and Marwa (7 trips)

6.4 Obligatory Acts (Wajibat) of Hajj
---
1. Ihram from the Miqat (designated starting point)
2. Staying at Arafat until sunset
3. Staying overnight at Muzdalifah
4. Staying overnight at Mina (Days of Tashreeq)
5. Stoning the Jamarat (pillars)
6. Shaving or trimming the hair
7. Tawaf al-Wada (farewell circumambulation)

CHAPTER 7: HALAL AND HARAM
═══════════════════════════════

"O mankind, eat from whatever is on earth [that is] lawful and good." (Quran 2:168)

7.1 General Principles
---
- Everything is Halal by default unless specifically prohibited
- Haram items are clearly stated in Quran and Sunnah
- Doubt: "Leave that which makes you doubt for that which does not make you doubt." (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2518)

7.2 Prohibited Foods
---
1. Dead animals (not slaughtered properly) — except fish and locusts
2. Blood that flows
3. Pork and all its derivatives
4. Animals slaughtered in a name other than Allah
5. Animals killed by strangling, beating, falling, goring, or wild animals — unless slaughtered before death
6. Alcohol and intoxicants — "Every intoxicant is Khamr and every Khamr is Haram." (Sahih Muslim 2003)

7.3 Islamic Slaughter (Dhabihah)
---
Requirements:
1. The slaughterer must be Muslim, or from the People of the Book (Christian/Jew)
2. The name of Allah must be mentioned: "Bismillah, Allahu Akbar"
3. The throat, windpipe, and blood vessels must be swiftly cut
4. The blood must be drained
5. A sharp instrument (not bones or nails) must be used

7.4 Financial Transactions
---
Prohibited:
- Riba (Interest/Usury) — "Allah has permitted trade and forbidden Riba." (Quran 2:275)
- Gharar (Excessive uncertainty in contracts)
- Maysir (Gambling)
- Selling what one does not own

Permitted alternatives:
- Murabaha (cost-plus financing)
- Musharakah (partnership)
- Mudarabah (profit-sharing)
- Ijarah (leasing)

CHAPTER 8: FAMILY LAW (NIKAH & TALAQ)
═══════════════════════════════

8.1 Marriage (Nikah)
---
"And among His signs is that He created for you mates from among yourselves, that you may dwell in tranquillity with them, and He has put love and mercy between your hearts." (Quran 30:21)

Pillars of marriage:
1. Proposal (Ijab) and acceptance (Qabul)
2. The bride's consent (her silence is consent for a virgin; explicit consent for a previously married woman)
3. Wali (guardian) — required by majority of scholars
4. Two witnesses
5. Mahr (dowry) — the bride's right; must be agreed upon

Rights of the wife:
- Mahr (dowry)
- Financial support (Nafaqah) — housing, food, clothing
- Kind and respectful treatment
- Privacy and dignity
- Right to education and knowledge

Rights of the husband:
- Obedience in what is good (Ma'ruf)
- Guarding his honour and wealth in his absence
- Kind treatment and companionship

8.2 Divorce (Talaq)
---
"Of all the lawful acts, the most detestable to Allah is divorce." (Abu Dawud 2178)

Types:
1. Talaq (Husband-initiated): Up to three pronouncements. The first two are revocable (Raj'i) during the waiting period ('Iddah).
2. Khul' (Wife-initiated): The wife returns her Mahr or part of it to obtain dissolution.
3. Faskh (Judicial annulment): A court may dissolve the marriage for valid reasons.

'Iddah (waiting period):
- Divorced woman: 3 menstrual cycles
- Widow: 4 months and 10 days
- Pregnant woman: until delivery

8.3 Inheritance (Mirath)
---
"For men is a share of what the parents and close relatives leave, and for women is a share of what the parents and close relatives leave." (Quran 4:7)

Fixed shares (Fard):
- Husband: 1/2 (no children) or 1/4 (with children)
- Wife: 1/4 (no children) or 1/8 (with children)
- Father: 1/6 (with children)
- Mother: 1/3 (no children, no siblings) or 1/6 (with children or 2+ siblings)
- Daughter: 1/2 (one) or 2/3 (two or more, no sons)
- Son: Residuary heir (Asabah) — receives what remains after fixed shares

The distribution follows precise Quranic injunctions (Quran 4:11–12) and established scholarly consensus.

CHAPTER 9: COMMON QUESTIONS (FAQ)
═══════════════════════════════

Q: Can I combine prayers?
A: Combining Dhuhr with Asr, or Maghrib with Isha is permitted during travel (Sahih Bukhari 1107). Some scholars also permit it for illness, heavy rain, or extreme difficulty.

Q: What invalidates my prayer?
A: Speaking intentionally, excessive unnecessary movement, turning away from the Qiblah, laughing, eating/drinking, and intentionally leaving a pillar (Rukn).

Q: Is it permissible to pray in English?
A: The Fard parts (Al-Fatiha, Tashahhud) must be in Arabic. Personal Dua within Sujud or after Tashahhud may be in any language (Hanbali view; debated in other madhabs).

Q: What is the ruling on music?
A: Majority of classical scholars (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) consider musical instruments Haram based on hadiths. The daff (hand drum) is permitted for weddings. Some modern scholars allow Nasheeds without instruments.

Q: Is cryptocurrency Halal?
A: This is a contemporary issue. A general principle: if the cryptocurrency is not used for Haram purposes and the transaction is free of Riba and Gharar, some scholars permit it. However, speculative trading may involve Gharar. Consultation with a qualified scholar is recommended. The Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions (AAOIFI) has issued guidelines.

Q: What is the ruling on Insurance?
A: Conventional insurance is generally considered Haram by most scholars due to elements of Gharar and Riba. Takaful (Islamic cooperative insurance) is the Shariah-compliant alternative. Basic mandatory insurance (e.g., car insurance required by law) is permitted under necessity (Darurah).
