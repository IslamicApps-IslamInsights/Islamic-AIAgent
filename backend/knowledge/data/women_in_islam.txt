WOMEN IN ISLAM — RIGHTS, ROLES, AND NOTABLE FIGURES
Source: Quran, Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, classical Islamic scholarship

CHAPTER 1: SPIRITUAL EQUALITY

"Whoever does righteousness, whether male or female, while being a believer — those will enter Paradise." (Quran 4:124)
"Indeed, the Muslim men and Muslim women, the believing men and believing women... for them Allah has prepared forgiveness and a great reward." (Quran 33:35)
"Women are the twin halves of men." (Abu Dawud 236, Tirmidhi 113)

Islam established: Women have independent spiritual identity. Each soul is accountable for its own deeds. No concept of inherited sin through Eve.

CHAPTER 2: RIGHTS GRANTED TO WOMEN

2.1 Right to Education and Knowledge
"Seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim." (Ibn Majah 224) — includes men and women.
Aisha (RA) was one of the greatest scholars of Islam, from whom even senior male companions would learn.
The Prophet ﷺ set aside a specific day for women's education.

2.2 Right to Own Property and Wealth
"For men is a share of what they have earned, and for women is a share of what they have earned." (Quran 4:32)
Women have full right to own, buy, sell, and manage property — independently from their husband or father.
Khadijah (RA) was a successful businesswoman before and after marriage.

2.3 Right to Inheritance
"For men is a share of what the parents and close relatives leave, and for women is a share of what the parents and close relatives leave." (Quran 4:7)
This was revolutionary in 7th-century Arabia where women received nothing.
The specific shares are detailed in Quran 4:11-12.

2.4 Right to Choose a Spouse
"A previously married woman has more right concerning herself than her guardian, and a virgin's consent must be sought." (Sahih Muslim 1421)
No woman can be forced into marriage against her will. Her consent is a condition of the marriage contract.

2.5 Right to Mahr (Dowry)
"And give the women their bridal gift graciously." (Quran 4:4)
Mahr is the woman's right — it belongs to her, not her family. She can use it as she wishes.

2.6 Right to Financial Support
The husband is obligated to provide housing, food, clothing, and medical care regardless of the wife's own wealth.
"Their rights upon you are that you should provide for them and clothe them in a reasonable manner." (Sahih Muslim 1218)

2.7 Right to Khul' (Divorce Initiative)
A woman may seek dissolution of marriage through Khul' by returning her Mahr.
The wife of Thabit ibn Qays came to the Prophet ﷺ and said she had no complaint about his character or religion but could not continue. The Prophet ﷺ instructed Thabit to accept the return of his garden (Mahr) and release her. (Sahih Bukhari 5273)

CHAPTER 3: NOTABLE WOMEN IN ISLAM

3.1 Khadijah bint Khuwaylid (RA)
- First person to accept Islam
- Supported the Prophet ﷺ emotionally, financially, and spiritually for 25 years
- Mother of all the Prophet's ﷺ surviving children
- The Prophet ﷺ said: "The best women of Paradise are Khadijah, Fatimah, Maryam, and Asiyah." (Musnad Ahmad)
- Jibreel sent her Salam (greetings) from Allah (Sahih Bukhari 3820)

3.2 Aisha bint Abu Bakr (RA)
- One of the greatest scholars of Islam
- Narrated over 2,200 hadiths
- Many senior companions would consult her on matters of Fiqh
- Described the Prophet's ﷺ character: "His character was the Quran." (Sahih Muslim 746)
- Expert in medicine, poetry, history, and Islamic law

3.3 Fatimah az-Zahra (RA)
- Daughter of the Prophet ﷺ
- "Leader of the women of Paradise" (Sahih Bukhari 3624)
- Known for extreme devotion, patience, and resemblance to the Prophet ﷺ in character

3.4 Maryam (Mary), Mother of Isa (AS)
- Only woman mentioned by name in the Quran
- An entire Surah (19) is named after her
- "And when the angels said, 'O Maryam, indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds.'" (Quran 3:42)

3.5 Asiyah, Wife of Pharaoh
- Believed despite being married to the worst tyrant
- "My Lord, build for me near You a house in Paradise." (Quran 66:11)
- A model of faith under extreme persecution

3.6 Other Notable Women:
- Sumayyah bint Khayyat (RA): First martyr of Islam
- Umm Salamah (RA): Her counsel at Hudaybiyyah saved the day
- Nusaybah bint Ka'b (RA): Fought bravely at Uhud, protecting the Prophet ﷺ
- Khawlah bint Tha'labah (RA): Argued her case before the Prophet ﷺ, and Allah revealed Surah Al-Mujadilah in response to her plea
- Rufaydah al-Aslamiyyah: First female nurse/surgeon in Islamic history, set up a field hospital in the Prophet's ﷺ mosque

CHAPTER 4: MODESTY (HIJAB)

"O Prophet, tell your wives and your daughters and the women of the believers to bring down over themselves their outer garments." (Quran 33:59)
"And tell the believing women to reduce their vision and guard their private parts and not expose their adornment except that which appears thereof." (Quran 24:31)

Scholarly understanding:
- Hijab is an act of worship and obedience to Allah
- It is the woman's choice and conviction (no compulsion in religion — Quran 2:256)
- It represents dignity, not oppression
- Men also have modesty obligations (lowering gaze, from navel to knee)

CHAPTER 5: COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS ADDRESSED

Misconception: "Islam oppresses women"
Reality: Islam gave women rights to property, inheritance, education, and consent 1,400 years ago — centuries before Western legislation.

Misconception: "Women's testimony is worth half of a man's"
Context: Quran 2:282 addresses a specific financial transaction context. In other matters (e.g., accusing a spouse — Quran 24:6-9), a woman's oath equals a man's.

Misconception: "Islam allows forced marriages"
Reality: "A virgin must not be married until her permission is sought." (Sahih Bukhari 5136). The Prophet ﷺ annulled marriages where women were married without consent.

Misconception: "Men can beat their wives"
Reality: Quran 4:34 uses the word "daraba" which scholars interpret in escalating steps of conflict resolution. The Prophet ﷺ never struck any woman. He said: "The best of you are the best to their families, and I am the best to my family." (Tirmidhi 3895)
